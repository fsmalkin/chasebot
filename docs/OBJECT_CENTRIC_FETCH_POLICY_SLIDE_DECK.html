A classroom-friendly robot story

Chasecar fetches a ball

We built a small robot that can practice finding a ball, driving to it, and helping it roll into a target circle.

Speaker note: Start with the simple mission. Chasecar is like a tiny mini-golf helper: see the ball, line up, touch gently, and measure whether the ball crossed the goal circle.
The mission
ball target pose
Easy to say, hard to do

Find it. Aim. Roll it. Check it.

The robot must be careful because a tiny bump can send the ball the wrong way.

  • 1Look for the ball.
  • 2Drive behind it.
  • 3Push on the ball-target line.
  • 4Watch if the ball reaches the circle.
Speaker note: Ask students why this is harder than it sounds. They may mention slippery floors, aiming, speed, and seeing where the ball is.

Walkie-talkies for code

Robot helpers talk in short messages

One helper reports the ball. Another reports position. Another plans a path. Another sends wheel speed commands.

  • EEyes: "I see the ball here."
  • MMap: "The robot is here."
  • PPlanner: "Drive this way."
  • WWheels: "Moving now through /cmd_vel."
Speaker note: ROS is like a classroom where everyone speaks on assigned channels, so the teacher can hear the right helper at the right time.

Six boxes run the mission

AI CoachLooks at a small state summary and suggests one next move.
Rule GuardChecks if the move is allowed and safe.

Speaker note: Emphasize that information loops around. The robot acts, senses what happened, then the system decides the next safe step.
AI and rules
LLM supervisor state digest
AI has a safe job

The AI is the coach, not the driver

AI

AI Coach

  • Reads a short summary of the mission.
  • Chooses one high-level next idea.
  • Can say search, acquire, plan, track, visit, or stop.
R

Rule Guard

  • Checks if that idea is on the allowed list.
  • Blocks stale, unsafe, or impossible commands.
  • Lets safe ideas continue to the executor.
Speaker note: The most important sentence: the AI suggests a play, but the rules decide whether that play can go onto the field.
Rules are the referee
frozen contract safe_stop
Safety comes first

Some ideas get a green light. Some get blocked.

Green light

  • The action is on the allowed list.
  • The robot has fresh sensor data.
  • The speed stays under the limit.
  • The mission is ready for that step.

Red light

  • The AI invents a command.
  • The target data is too old.
  • The robot is not ready.
  • Stopping is safer than moving.
Speaker note: This is the crossing-guard idea. The rules are boring on purpose, because boring safety rules are exactly what robots need.

When the ball matters most

The robot thinks about the ball, the target, and itself

1SetupGet behind the ball on the line to the target.
2ContactUse a bounded push, not a wild shove.
3ObserveWatch where the ball rolls.
4RecoverRe-aim if there is time and it is safe.
5DoneAccept when the ball path reaches the circle.
Speaker note: This is the current fetch-to-pose idea. The robot does not only chase the ball; it reasons about the relationship between robot, ball, and target circle.

Robots need report cards

Every run teaches us something

We save what happened, compare it to the goal, and only promote ideas that pass repeatable checks.

Pass or failDid the ball path reach the circle?
How close?Measure the closest approach to the target.
Was it stable?Check wobble, side drift, and first contact.
Can it repeat?Run the same layout again and again.
Speaker note: Link this to science class. Good robot work is not just "it worked once"; it is measured, repeated, and written down.
The big idea
AI + rules teamwork
What to remember

Smart robots are teams

AI helps choose. Rules keep it safe. Sensors tell the truth. Controllers move the wheels. Tests prove what happened.

  • 1Do not let AI drive the wheels directly.
  • 2Give AI safe choices and clear facts.
  • 3Measure the robot like a science experiment.
  • 4Improve one layer at a time.
Speaker note: Close with the core lesson: a safe robot is not one giant brain. It is a careful team where every part has a job.
